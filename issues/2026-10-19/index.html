Lead story · Anthropic

Anthropic puts 5% of its shares into a public benefit fund that grows as the company does

Subject to stockholder approval, 5% of Anthropic’s shares go into a new Anthropic Public Benefit Fund, with another 2% possible at the board’s discretion for every 50% rise in value above a set baseline. It’s a wholly owned LLC rather than a 501(c)(3), so it can switch between big donations to nonprofits and “direct distributions to specific populations” — AI’s impact on jobs is the named example, on top of the $200M Anthropic has already committed to that. None of the equity can go to lobbying or political giving. Add the co-founders’ 80% pledge and the employee match, and Anthropic says roughly 20% of its shares are now set aside for the public good. It landed Sunday as the company lays the ground for a possible blockbuster IPO; Musk’s reply was to give the shares directly to the public.

Only about a third of the US data-centre pipeline looks buildable, FT reports

At least 47 large US projects have been delayed, withdrawn or blocked this year, more than 1,000 communities have sought limits, and analysts reckon only about a third of the planned pipeline is credible. Credit is turning too: a CoreWeave unsecured bond slid into distressed territory with its shares down almost 25% on the week, and SB Energy shelved a $50bn listing. Robotics

Altman: OpenAI “will” build a humanoid, with “a cool demo in 2027”

On the Sources podcast Altman said a human shape makes sense because “the world is very much designed for people”, but the real work is “the brain that makes the robot work”. He told Fortune not to expect robots walking the streets for a few more years. OpenAI’s job posts say the short-term aim is general-purpose robots that help skilled workers build infrastructure, with a personal robot as the long-term goal.

— Engadget · engadget.com
Research

Hybrid Reasoning Agents think in latent space and switch to text only to act

Stanford, UIUC and Together AI researchers add a small adapter (HybridLink, about 10M parameters on a 4B model) that lets an agent reason in hidden states and drop back to text for tool calls. On Qwen3 at 4B–14B across 11 benchmarks they report +10.4% performance, 35.5–54.7% fewer tokens and 1.8–2.6× faster inference — and the latent steps can still be read back as pseudo-text for auditing. It topped Hacker News this morning.

— arXiv · arxiv.org
Finland

First SOTE AI Hackathon names its winners in Espoo

Länsi-Uusimaa wellbeing services county ran the two-day event at Dipoli around three real problems: freeing professionals’ time for clients, using up-to-date client data, and building an agentic wellbeing companion. Winners were Anamnesis (patient history from scattered systems in one traceable view, built during the event), Microsoft, Team AKP and Kide. Each got €1,500 plus LUMI AI Factory compute, and the county wants to turn it into a national model with Sitra.

— Tivi · tivi.fi
