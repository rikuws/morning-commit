Lead story · Meta

Meta's Muse Wonder lands at the frontier — and level with Claude Code on coding agents

Meta Superintelligence Labs released Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full on 12 Oct, pitched at personal and professional agents with 'Active Memory' and 'Active Focus'. Artificial Analysis scores them 52 and 56 on its Intelligence Index (Muse Spark 1.3 was 48), with Terminal-Bench up from 33% to 57% for Full. Muse Wonder is on Meta's API at $4/$20 per million input/output tokens with a 1M context; Full is only inside Muse Code for now. A fuller safety report is still to come.

Research

DeepMind's AlphaProof Nexus cracks open Erdős problems — peer reviewed in Science

Built on Gemini 3.1 Pro drafting proofs that Lean checks step by step, it solved 9 of 353 open Erdős problems (two open for 56 years) and proved 44 OEIS conjectures. Unlike OpenAI's recent proof dump on GitHub, this went through peer review; the proofs are public.

— The Decoder · the-decoder.com
Industry

Musk: only OpenAI, Anthropic and Cursor have enough coding data

Asked why SpaceX paid about $60B for Cursor, Musk said cracking coding is the path to superintelligence and named just three organisations with enough programming training data — Google 'barely half', xAI not at all. Same day, Broadcom's and Intel's CEOs called capital, not chips, the industry's biggest constraint.

— Silicon Noon · siliconnoon.com
Dev tools

agent-engineer: an open plugin that teaches coding agents to build agents

Sarthak Rastogi's free plugin for Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, OpenCode and Gemini CLI adds 11 skills, 4 subagents and 5 hooks on evals, tool safety, RAG and guardrails. In his own blinded A/B on six tasks, Opus 5.5 met 53% of requirements with it vs 34% without — small sample, his numbers.

— AI Agent Engineering · sarthakai.substack.com
Finland

ICEYE and Nokia team up on sovereign satellite networks

Helsinki's ICEYE will pair its sovereign SAR satellite missions with Nokia's secure networking to build LEO communications that governments own outright — for defence, border security and disaster response when ground networks fail. First satellites are expected in 2028.
