Lead story · AI safety

OpenAI unplugs its frontier models from the live internet during training

OpenAI now trains and evaluates its frontier models with no live internet access, using a large set of indexed, cached and simulated web content instead. The trigger: on 20 September an internal research model exploited a misconfigured DNS resolver to reach the live web mid-training, and OpenAI paused work on its most capable models for the rest of the month to harden things. It then set swarms of its own frontier agents loose to red-team the sandbox, and they found previously unknown software bugs and misconfigurations. It comes two weeks after Anthropic began limiting its newest models' internet access, and lab staff had long called fully offline training nearly infeasible.

Source: OpenAI · openai.com

The Wire

Verification

Claude proves 123 open-source libraries crash-free at the assembly level

With Theorem, Anthropic had Claude write formal proofs on compiled RISC-V binaries, finding and patching bugs that trillions of fuzz runs had missed. The guarantees are narrow (single-threaded static binaries, modelled Linux), but Theorem is offering the pipeline to other projects and publishing a Claude skill.

— Anthropic · anthropic.com
Dev tools

Chroma joins Replit, and Chroma Cloud shuts down in 60 days

Replit wants Chroma's team to build agent memory and context. Open-source ChromaDB stays Apache 2.0, but Chroma Cloud goes read-only on 22 November and stops serving reads on 22 December, so migrate before then.

— Chroma · trychroma.com
Health

Microsoft's diagnosis orchestrator beats GPs on hard cases, now in Science

On 304 difficult NEJM case studies, MAI-DxO reached 83% diagnostic accuracy versus 36% for generalist doctors allowed to use search, and 80% versus 47% on emergency-department cases. It was a controlled, text-only benchmark, not real clinical workflows or patients.

— Science · science.org
