Lead story · Research

Two labs, one week: OpenAI and Anthropic both say their AI disproved the Exponential Time Hypothesis

OpenAI announced three results on Monday: a proof of the local smoothing conjecture in every dimension (which also settles Kakeya), an existence proof for QCD and Yang–Mills in four dimensions, and a refutation of ETH, the ~30-year-old belief that 3-SAT needs exponential time, used in hundreds of hardness papers. Its model found the ETH disproof on 16 October and it was checked in Lean the next day; ETH's original proposers Impagliazzo and Paturi, with Ryan Williams, will lead the independent check. Hours later Anthropic posted its own paper, "A 2^o(n) Time Algorithm for 3-SAT", saying an internal Claude model found the core algorithm, also on 16 October, with 14M output tokens and no human input, also formalized in Lean. It's theoretical: the speedup is subexponential, not a practical SAT solver, and no human has fully checked either proof yet.

Out in ChatGPT Work, Codex and the API, priced 50% below GPT-5.6 Terra. OpenAI says it scores 50.3% on Terminal-Bench 4.0 at max effort (vs 21.5% for GPT-5.6 Terra) and costs about 9x less per task than Claude Opus 5.5 on Agents' Last Exam. Benchmarks are OpenAI's own.

Security

Cloudflare's GLM-5.3 leaked other users' data into 155 responses via a KV cache bug

Prompts over 681,280 tokens overflowed the prefill KV pool; an SGLang bug in disaggregated mode then let decode read stale pages from earlier requests and return HTTP 200. Fixed in September, disclosed now. If you run disaggregated SGLang, upgrade to v0.5.22 and check pool size against context length.
