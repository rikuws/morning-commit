ANTHROPIC

Anthropic's FlashProve wants to prove which model actually wrote your answer

Anthropic researchers published FlashProve, an open-source system for "provable inference": a cryptographic proof that a given output came from a specific model. Labs would publish a fingerprint for each model that doesn't reveal its weights, so it could work for closed models too, and verification is meant to be light enough to run on your own device. The point is to catch a provider quietly swapping in a cheaper model, or a pricier one to pad benchmark results. The spec and working code are on GitHub, but it's still research, with no plans yet to deploy it at Anthropic or anywhere else.

Source: TechCrunch · techcrunch.com

RESEARCH

LLMs reading raw weights: Simon Willison gets Qwen 3.8 27B on a laptop to do it too

MIT CSAIL's "weight-space programming" post had GPT-6 Astra tell what small networks were trained on, and edit 0.016% of Qwen3.5-0.8B's weights to lift MATH-500 from 7.9% to 48.1%. Willison reran the no-tools game test locally: Qwen named Pac-Man, Pong and Breakout (taking 46 to 65 minutes each) but missed Freeway. He's impressed, but not ready to call it the end of source code.

— Simon Willison · simonwillison.net
SECURITY

CrowdStrike pays nearly $500M for two startups that watch and test AI agents

Fiddler AI (about $400M, agent observability) becomes Falcon Observe, and Dreadnode (about $95M, automated security testing of models and agents) feeds Falcon Guardian. The bet: companies are rolling out agents faster than their security teams can keep track of them.

EDUCATION

Google will switch off AI for K-12 students unless schools opt in, but blocking AI also blocks Search

The change covers Gemini, NotebookLM, Gemini in Classroom and Search. Google says Search and AI are now too intertwined to split, so a school that turns AI off completely will see students get a "not available" message instead of Google Search.

— TechCrunch · techcrunch.com
FINLAND

Linus Torvalds: AI patches are finally landing as normal development, not a flood of fixes

The latest Linux merge window has nearly 20,000 changes versus about 15,000 usually. Torvalds is happy AI-made patches no longer pile up as extra fixes during the -rc phase, though he notes AMD GPU register dumps (about a third) and 7.4 being the expected LTS also swell it.
